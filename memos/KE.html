Kimball Electronics: a regulated-market manufacturer priced at book value

Thesis

Kimball Electronics builds electronics for regulated, high-reliability end markets: medical devices, automotive braking and steering systems, and industrial controls. Qualification cycles in these markets run years, so programs are sticky once won. The stock trades at 0.42x sales and 1.04x book value of $24.02 per share, a steep discount to EMS peers like Plexus and Benchmark at 0.6x to 1.0x sales.

The market cap fell from $735M in January 2026 to a March trough near $567M on cyclical auto softness. We think that move overshot. The medical and industrial franchise stayed durable through the correction: Q3 FY26 medical sales grew 10 percent sequentially, and management affirmed full-year guidance at the high end of its 4.2 to 4.5 percent adjusted operating margin range.

The business generates cash through the cycle. Kimball has posted 9 straight quarters of positive operating cash flow and $55M of trailing free cash flow, $2.30 per share against a $25 stock. We do not need a boom. We need the auto inventory correction to end while book value protects the downside.

Business

Kimball is a contract electronics manufacturer headquartered in Jasper, Indiana, with plants in the US, Mexico, Thailand, Poland, and China. It targets markets where failure is unacceptable: automotive safety electronics, medical devices, and industrial controls. Customers qualify Kimball into multi-year programs, and switching costs keep those programs in place.

The company divested its non-core AT&M test and measurement unit, which sharpened the pure-play EMS story. It then announced the EUR 90M acquisition of Helvoet Polymer, a European medical components maker expected to be accretive in FY2027. A new 300,000 square foot Indianapolis medical contract-manufacturing facility comes online by year-end. Both moves push the mix toward medical, the segment growing right now. Trailing revenue is $1.44B, down 6.3 percent, which confirms the auto-driven cyclical softness rather than a franchise problem.

Why the market is wrong

Screens see a shrinking-revenue manufacturer with thin margins and a trailing P/E of 23.9 on depressed earnings. Only 3 to 4 analysts cover the name. Nobody separates the cyclical auto book from the growing medical book, so the whole company gets priced off the weakest segment at the bottom of its inventory cycle.

Our variant view is that the medical franchise deserves a peer multiple and the auto book is troughing. Q3 FY26 sales of $353M rose 3.4 percent sequentially with medical up 10 percent, and guidance held at $1.40 to 1.46B. Meanwhile the balance sheet backs the price: book value sits at $24.02 per share against a $25.09 stock. A buyer here pays roughly nothing for the earnings recovery, the Helvoet accretion, or the new Indianapolis capacity. The share count is stable at 24.05M with a modest 1 percent buyback, so no dilution eats the recovery.

Valuation

At $25.09 the stock trades at 0.42x sales, 1.04x book, 7.1x EV/EBITDA, and 17.9x forward earnings. One correction to our original work: enterprise value is $700M, so EV/sales is 0.49x, higher than the 0.4x in our first draft. The gap comes from around $97M of net debt ($180M debt against $82M cash), partly from the Helvoet acquisition the original thesis missed. The acquisition itself strengthens the medical story, but readers should use 0.49x.

Even at 0.49x EV/sales, Kimball sits well below the 0.6x to 1.0x peer range. A re-rate to 0.6x sales implies a stock in the low 30s, near the analyst target of $33 and the 52-week high of $33.19. On earnings, forward EPS around $1.40 at a 15x multiple gets to a similar place. Downside anchors at book value of $24.02, about 4 percent below the current price. We see 30 percent upside against limited downside.

Risks

Automotive concentration is the risk that kills this thesis. A prolonged auto electronics inventory correction, or the loss of a top-5 program, would hit plant utilization hard, and EMS operating margins near 4.5 percent leave little cushion. Revenue is already down 6.3 percent; a second leg down would push earnings toward zero and test the book-value floor.

The balance sheet carries more debt than our first pass showed: $97M net debt, not net cash, after Helvoet. Integration of a European acquisition adds execution risk. The new Indianapolis facility creates a 40 to 50 basis point gross margin headwind in FY2027 before it fills. The trailing P/E of 23.9 means the stock is only cheap if earnings recover; if trough earnings are the new normal, the multiple is not a bargain. Coverage is thin, so a re-rate could take years.

Catalysts

Q4 FY26 earnings on August 12, 2026 will show whether medical momentum held and whether management guides FY2027 with Helvoet accretion included. Helvoet closes in 2026 and adds accretive medical revenue in FY2027. The Indianapolis medical facility comes online by year-end and ramps through FY2027. Any sign the auto inventory correction has ended restores the cyclical earnings leg. Analysts already carry a $33 target, so a recovery needs no re-rating beyond what the 3 to 4 covering firms model today.
