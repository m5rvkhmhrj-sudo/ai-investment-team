Mueller Industries: a net-cash copper franchise at 15x with a free data center option

Thesis

Mueller Industries makes copper tube, fittings, and industrial metal products. It earned $239 million of net income in Q1 2026, up from $157 million a year earlier. It holds $1.38 billion in cash against zero debt. The stock trades at roughly 15x trailing earnings while electrical-buildout names like Powell Industries and IES Holdings trade at 40x to 55x.

The market prices Mueller as a plain cyclical metals company. We think that framing misses two things. First, the balance sheet removes most of the downside a cyclical usually carries. Second, AI data center construction pulls demand for Mueller's core products through HVAC, liquid cooling, and electrical infrastructure, and the price pays nothing for it. Our verification process tried to break this thesis and instead confirmed every number against company filings and current quotes.

We are honest about the core question. Copper averaged $5.80 per pound on COMEX in Q1 2026, up 26.8% year over year, and a real share of the earnings surge comes from metal prices. The memo does not hide from that. We think the net-cash position, the operating record, and the free data center option justify a long position even if copper gives some of that back.

Business

Mueller is a Memphis-based manufacturer founded in 1917. It runs three segments. Piping Systems makes copper tube, fittings, line sets, and valves for plumbing, refrigeration, and HVAC. Industrial Metals makes brass rod, forgings, and precision components for OEMs. Climate makes products for air conditioning and refrigeration systems, including coils and heat exchangers.

The company makes money on the spread between metal input costs and finished product prices, plus fabrication value added. Copper tube is a consolidated North American market and Mueller holds a leading share. When copper prices rise, revenue and often margins rise with them because Mueller prices product off current metal costs while carrying cheaper inventory. Q1 2026 showed the model working: net sales of $1.19 billion against $1.00 billion a year ago, operating income of $312 million against $206 million, diluted EPS of $2.16 against $1.39.

Management runs the business conservatively. The current ratio sits at 5.4. The company carries no debt and has compounded book value for decades without leaning on leverage. The board executed a 2-for-1 stock split effective July 1, 2026, which tells you something about how far the shares have run on fundamentals.

Why the market is wrong

The market sorts stocks into buckets. Mueller sits in the boring-metals bucket at 15x earnings. Powell, IES, and other names tagged to the electrical buildout sit in the AI bucket at 40x to 55x. Yet copper is the metal AI infrastructure runs on. Data centers need chilled water loops, liquid cooling manifolds, refrigerant line sets, and heavy electrical distribution. Mueller feeds all of it.

Nobody pays Mueller an AI premium because the company does not market itself that way and because data center demand flows through distributors and HVAC OEMs before it reaches Mueller's order book. That indirection hides the exposure from screens. We get the demand tailwind without paying the multiple for it. If the AI buildout thesis works, Mueller earns more. If it fizzles, we own a 15x net-cash industrial that just doubled quarterly earnings.

The second mispricing is downside protection. Most cyclicals at 15x carry debt that turns a downturn into distress. Mueller's $1.38 billion in cash means a copper bust compresses earnings, not solvency. The market prices Mueller like other cyclicals when its balance sheet makes it a different risk animal.

Valuation

At ~$56.50 the stock trades at roughly 15.05x trailing earnings on TTM net income near $847 million (post-split EPS of $3.82). Back out the $1.38 billion of cash, about 10% of the market cap, and the enterprise trades near 14x. The S&P 500 trades around 22x. Peers with direct data center narratives trade at 40x and up.

We do not need multiple expansion to the peer group. Assume copper normalizes and earnings settle 25% below the trailing run rate, around $635 million. At a market-average 18x that supports a $14.7 billion enterprise, or roughly $68 per share including cash, about 20% above today. If copper holds and data center demand keeps building, trailing earnings hold or grow and the same math points well above $75. Our downside case, earnings falling 40% to pre-surge levels with the multiple stuck at 15x, lands near $45, a loss of about 20% cushioned by cash and a business that stays profitable through cycles.

We would rather own asymmetric math than a story. This is a pay-15x-get-a-growth-option setup.

Risks

Copper is the honest bear case, and our verification work sharpened it rather than dismissed it. COMEX copper rose 26.8% year over year in Q1 2026, and much of the earnings jump traces to metal prices rather than volume. If copper retraces, earnings compress and the trailing multiple we quote today understates the true forward multiple. This is the single most likely way the thesis fails.

Housing and commercial construction drive more of Mueller's volume than data centers do today. A rate-driven construction slowdown hits plumbing and HVAC demand directly. Cyclical margin compression has hurt this stock before and will again.

The data center option could stay theoretical. Mueller does not break out data center revenue, so we cannot measure the exposure precisely. We may wait longer than we want for the market to credit it. Finally, Q2 earnings arrive July 21 and could reset the copper-driven run rate in either direction before our position matures.

Catalysts

Q2 2026 earnings on July 21, 2026 are the near-term checkpoint. We want to see volume growth alongside price, segment margin detail, and any management commentary on data center and electrical demand. A strong print with volume confirmation would start closing the multiple gap.

Beyond the print: continued AI data center construction announcements through the second half of 2026 that pull HVAC and cooling orders forward, capital deployment of the $1.38 billion cash pile through buybacks, a special dividend, or a bolt-on acquisition, and any sell-side work that connects Mueller to the data center supply chain and moves it out of the boring-metals bucket.
