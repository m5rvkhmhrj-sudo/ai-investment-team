Thesis

Crawford & Company is the largest independent claims adjuster and third-party administrator in the world. Insurance carriers and self-insured corporates pay it per claim to investigate, adjust, and administer losses. Crawford takes no underwriting risk. It earns fees on the volume of claims that flow through the insurance system.

The stock trades at $11.48 with a market cap near $560M. Our verification pass found the stock cheaper than the original screen claimed: forward P/E sits around 10.5 to 11.9x, not the 12.9x we first wrote down, and enterprise value is $692M to $738M, not $755M. Comparable claims and TPA assets have transacted privately at far higher EBITDA multiples, and the stock trades below its own 10-year average multiple.

We think the dual-class structure, family control, and micro-cap size keep institutions out. That neglect, plus a fee-based book insurers cannot easily replace, gives us a business worth more than the market pays. We size the position modestly because earnings depend on catastrophe activity more than a pure fee-based framing implies.

Business

Crawford, founded in 1941 and based in Atlanta, runs 3 main businesses. North America Loss Adjusting and International Operations handle field adjusting for property and casualty carriers, including large and complex commercial losses. Broadspire administers claims for self-insured corporates, mostly workers compensation, liability, and medical management, on multi-year contracts. Platform Solutions handles catastrophe surge staffing and contractor networks.

In Q1 2026 revenue was $309.5M, down 1 percent. The mix tells the story. International grew 4.5 percent to $131.9M and Broadspire grew 1.0 percent to $104.8M, both steady. US P&C fell 11.3 percent to $72.9M because US catastrophe activity ran quiet. The company carries $54.5M in cash and a debt-to-equity ratio around 155 percent, and it pays a 2.67 percent dividend yield.

Why the market is wrong

Screens see a trailing P/E of 31.9 on TTM EPS of $0.36 and move on. Thin GAAP margins inflate that multiple. On forward earnings the stock costs 10.5 to 11.9x, and on EV to EBITDA it sits below where private buyers have paid for claims and TPA assets. The dual-class share structure and the Crawford family's control block the stock from most indexes and many institutional mandates, so almost nobody does this work.

The durable core is Broadspire and International, which together produced 76 percent of Q1 revenue and grew through a weak catastrophe quarter. Carriers have spent 2 decades outsourcing claims handling, and Crawford's scale, licensing footprint, and carrier relationships take years to replicate. We also note the takeout-multiple gap: private equity has paid up for TPA platforms, and Crawford trades well below those marks. Family control blocks a sale today, which is exactly why the discount exists and why patient public buyers get the asset cheap.

Valuation

At $11.48 the stock trades at roughly 10.5 to 11.9x forward EPS and an enterprise value of $692M to $738M. Both figures come from our July 2026 verification and replace the softer numbers in our original screen, which cited a 12.9x forward P/E and a $755M EV. The corrected numbers make the stock cheaper, not more expensive.

A re-rate to 14x forward earnings, still below the 10-year average and far below private TPA transaction multiples, puts the stock in the $13 to $15 range. Street targets run $11 to $14, with Truist at Buy and Barrington at Outperform. The 2.67 percent dividend pays us while we wait. A normal catastrophe year restores the US P&C segment and lifts the forward number our multiple sits on.

Risks

Catastrophe cyclicality is the honest flaw in this thesis. Our original framing called revenue durable because Crawford earns fees, not underwriting profit. Verification pushed back: earnings are cyclical to CAT activity. Q1 2026 proved it, with adjusted EPS of $0.16 against $0.21 a year ago and adjusted EBITDA of $22.4M against $26.8M, driven by an 11.3 percent drop in US P&C revenue. A second quiet CAT year plus wage inflation in adjuster labor compresses earnings fast.

Leverage compounds that risk. Debt to equity near 155 percent is high for a business we pitch as fee-based, and it thins the margin for error in a weak year. The dual-class structure cuts both ways: it creates the discount we buy, and it blocks the takeout premium that would close it. Our verification confidence on this name was medium, not high, so we hold it at lower conviction than our top picks. If Broadspire growth stalls while CAT stays quiet, the cheap multiple stops mattering and this becomes dead money.

Catalysts

Q2 2026 earnings in early August 2026 show whether US CAT activity normalized; hurricane season runs June through November and any active season feeds revenue within a quarter or two. Continued Broadspire and International growth keeps compounding the stable 3-quarters of the business. Deleveraging from free cash flow would shrink the 155 percent debt-to-equity overhang and support a re-rate. Any TPA or claims-services transaction at a rich multiple resets the comp set the market uses for Crawford, even with family control blocking a sale of the company itself.
