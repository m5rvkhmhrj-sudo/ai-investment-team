InMode: 4x earnings ex-cash for a business the market left for dead

Thesis

InMode trades at $14.79 with $537.2M of cash, marketable securities, and deposits on the balance sheet as of March 31, 2026. That cash pile covers around 63 percent of the $850M market cap. Trailing EPS of roughly $1.43 puts the stock at 10.3x earnings, and the operating business trades near 4x earnings once you strip out the cash.

The market prices InMode as a melting ice cube: an aesthetics equipment maker whose customers stopped buying. That story just broke. Q1 2026 revenue of $82M grew 5 percent year over year, and management reiterated full-year guidance of $365M to $375M, roughly flat against 2025's $370.5M. Revenue has stabilized. The stabilization was the exact catalyst our original thesis waited for, and it already arrived.

Meanwhile the company keeps shrinking the float. It returned $127.4M through buybacks in 2025 and repurchases continue in 2026. We do not need growth. We need flat revenue, continued cash generation, and a multiple that stops pricing terminal decline.

Business

InMode is an Israeli medical device company that builds radio-frequency platforms for minimally invasive aesthetic procedures. Its systems, including Morpheus8, BodyTite, and Optimas, sit between injectables and full surgery: skin tightening, body contouring, and facial remodeling without a scalpel. Customers are plastic surgeons, dermatologists, and med spas who buy capital equipment and then purchase consumables per procedure.

The model carries software-like economics for a hardware business. Non-GAAP gross margins run 74 to 76 percent, and the company generates cash every quarter; Q1 2026 operating cash flow was $15.4M. InMode holds no debt and has expanded into women's health and ophthalmology platforms to widen the addressable market beyond core aesthetics.

Why the market is wrong

The bear narrative says GLP-1 drugs and tight consumer credit killed med-spa spending, so aesthetics capital equipment demand shrinks forever. The stock fell from over $16 toward its lows on that story, and it now trades below half its 5-year average multiple. Trailing P/E sits near 10.3x against medtech peers at 20x to 25x and against InMode's own history above 20x.

The data no longer supports the narrative. Revenue grew 5 percent in Q1 2026 after a 6 percent decline in 2025. Guidance for 2026 implies flat revenue, and management held that guidance after Q1. A business at 4x ex-cash earnings needs terminal decline to justify its price. Flat is enough to win. Every quarter of stabilization plus buybacks compounds the gap between price and value, and the cash pile puts a hard floor under the stock.

Valuation

Trailing EPS runs about $1.43, so the stock trades at 10.3x. Back out the $537.2M of cash and investments and the operating business costs around $315M, near 4x earnings. Management guides 2026 non-GAAP EPS of $1.33 to $1.38, a forward P/E around 10.8x to 11x on the full market cap.

A re-rate to 15x forward earnings, still a steep discount to medtech peers, puts the stock near $20, roughly 35 percent upside. The buyback does work even without a re-rate: retiring shares at 4x ex-cash earnings raises per-share value fast. Our original screen cited gross margins near 80 percent; the correct figure is 74 to 76 percent guided for 2026. The cheapness case survives the correction with room to spare.

Risks

The value trap scenario is the main risk. If GLP-1 adoption and weak consumer credit keep suppressing med-spa capital spending, 1 quarter of growth means nothing and revenue resumes shrinking. Q1 could prove a false dawn.

Margin erosion is the residual risk our verification flagged. Q1 2026 non-GAAP EPS of $0.25 missed the $0.30 estimate on tariff costs and mix, and the cash balance fell from $555.3M at year-end 2025 to $537.2M, partly from buybacks. Our verification also corrected the original gross margin claim from near 80 percent down to the guided 74 to 76 percent. Israel-based operations add geopolitical and headline risk that can cap the multiple regardless of results. Finally, a large idle cash pile invites capital allocation mistakes; a bad acquisition would burn the exact cushion we underwrite.

Catalysts

Q2 2026 earnings in late July or early August is the near-term test: a second straight quarter of year-over-year growth kills the melting-ice-cube narrative. Reiterated or raised full-year guidance of $365M to $375M confirms stabilization. Continued buybacks at these prices shrink the float against a cash pile covering 63 percent of the market cap. Any clarity on tariff mitigation restores the margin story and the $0.05 of quarterly EPS the Q1 miss cost. A special dividend or accelerated repurchase from the $537M cash balance would force the market to price the operating business on its own.
