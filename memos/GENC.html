Thesis

Gencor Industries holds $155.1M in cash and marketable securities with zero debt. The whole company trades near $215M. The market therefore values the operating business, which earned about $13.0M net income over the trailing 12 months through March 31, 2026, at roughly $52M to $65M. That is an ex-cash P/E of 4x to 5x for a profitable manufacturer with a demand floor set by federal highway spending.

Shareholders' equity stood at $219.1M on March 31, 2026, so the stock trades at about 1.0x book while more than 70 percent of that book sits in cash and securities. Peer Astec Industries trades above 2x book. Backlog more than doubled year over year, from $27.8M to $60.5M, which points to a stronger back half of fiscal 2026.

Our original screen cited $15.7M net income on $115M revenue as trailing figures. Verification corrected this: those were fiscal 2025 numbers (year ended September 30, 2025). True TTM figures through March 31, 2026 are $103.2M revenue and about $13.0M net income. The correction trims the cheapness claim without breaking it. Ex-cash P/E still lands at 4x to 5x.

Business

Gencor, based in Orlando, Florida, manufactures heavy machinery for highway construction. Its core products are hot-mix asphalt plants sold under the Bituma and H&B brands, plus combustion systems, thermal fluid heaters, and related environmental equipment. Customers are road builders and asphalt producers who buy plants in lumpy, multi-million-dollar orders tied to paving demand.

Federal highway funding drives that demand. The federal surface transportation program keeps state DOT paving budgets funded, and asphalt producers replace or expand plants when road work runs hot. Q2 FY2026 (quarter ended March 31, 2026) showed revenue of $33.8M, down 11.5 percent year over year, net income of $3.8M, down 37 percent, EPS of $0.26, and gross margin of 31.7 percent. A $3.5M trade show expense (ConExpo, held every 3 years) drove much of the earnings drop. The $60.5M backlog is the forward signal that matters more than the soft quarter.

Why the market is wrong

Nobody looks at this stock. No sell-side analyst covers it, the float is thin, and the Marino family controls the company through dual-class shares. Screens show a full-price 16x headline P/E because they count the cash pile in the market cap while the cash earns almost nothing toward the numerator. Strip the cash and the operating business trades at 4x to 5x earnings against its own 10-year average near 10x.

The market also punished a governance stumble. Gencor withdrew its May 8, 2026 preliminary Q2 release pending a financial review, drew an NYSE filing delinquency notice, and reissued results on June 12, 2026. The reissued numbers confirmed the originals. We think investors sold the headline and never read the resolution. A confirmed balance sheet with $155M of cash and no debt, priced as if the review found something, is the mispricing we want.

Valuation

Start with the balance sheet. Cash and marketable securities of $155.1M against a $207M to $220M market cap leaves an implied enterprise value of roughly $52M to $65M. TTM net income of about $13.0M puts the ex-cash multiple at 4x to 5x. The stock also trades at 0.95x to 1.0x a book value that is mostly liquid assets marked at or near market.

A modest re-rate does the work. At 8x ex-cash earnings, still below the 10-year average of 10x, the operating business is worth about $104M. Add the cash and the total reaches roughly $260M, or near $17.70 per share, about 20 to 25 percent above the current range and just above the 52-week high. If the doubled backlog converts and earnings recover toward the $15.7M fiscal 2025 level, the same math supports $20 or more. Downside protection comes from the cash: at $14.11 the operating business costs $52M, or about 0.5x TTM revenue, for a 30-percent-gross-margin manufacturer.

Risks

The cash may never reach us. The Marino family controls Gencor through dual-class shares, pays no dividend, and runs no buyback. The pile can sit idle indefinitely, and a stock at 1.0x book with dead capital can stay there for years. This is the main way the thesis fails: we own the discount and nobody ever closes it.

Governance carries a live caveat. The withdrawn Q2 release and the NYSE delinquency notice ended cleanly, with figures confirmed on June 12, 2026, but an accounting review at a family-controlled nano-cap deserves a haircut, and we apply one. Our verification also rated overall confidence medium, not high, because the original screen missed this episode entirely and misdated the earnings figures. Beyond governance, revenue is lumpy and tied to asphalt plant order cycles. Q2 revenue fell 11.5 percent and net income fell 37 percent. A federal highway funding lapse or a paving downturn would hit orders hard. The stock is thinly traded, so building or exiting a position moves the price.

Catalysts

Q3 FY2026 results, due around August 2026, should show backlog conversion. The $60.5M backlog, up from $27.8M a year earlier, has to flow through revenue in the June and September quarters, and clean prints will also rebuild trust after the May withdrawal episode. Fiscal 2025 full-year results arrive in December 2026 and give the market a fresh look at normalized earnings without the $3.5M trade show expense. Any capital return decision, a first dividend or a buyback, would re-rate the stock fast given that cash covers about 72 percent of the market cap. Reauthorization debates over federal surface transportation funding ahead of the current bill's expiration keep highway demand in the headlines through 2026.
