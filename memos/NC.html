Thesis

NACCO Industries trades at $48.40 against $57.97 of book value per share, or 0.83x book. The market sees a coal company and prices in terminal decline. We see something different: management contracts that pay fees on coal other people dig, with almost no NACCO capital at risk, plus a growing royalty segment layered on top.

The earnings turn is already visible. Q1 2026 EPS came in at $1.17, up 80 percent year over year, and operating profit rose 43 percent to $11.0M. The company raised its dividend 4 percent in May 2026 and holds a $20M Class A buyback authorization through December 2027. Total equity of $437.1M backs a $365M market cap.

We do not need coal to grow. We need the fee contracts to run their remaining life while royalties and contract mining expand, and we collect the gap between price and book plus a 2.1 percent yield while we wait.

Business

NACCO, based in Cleveland, Ohio, operates 3 main segments. The Coal Mining segment runs lignite mines that feed adjacent power plants, mostly through unconsolidated subsidiaries. Those subsidiaries earn a management fee per ton delivered, and the utility customer funds the capital and carries the reserves. NACCO collects the fee stream with minimal balance sheet exposure.

North American Mining extends the same contract model to aggregates and other minerals, and it is growing. Volumes rose 16 percent, helped by a new dragline project for the Army Corps of Engineers. The Minerals Management segment owns oil, gas, and mineral royalty interests, another fee-like stream that requires no operating capital. A small Mitigation Resources unit builds environmental mitigation credits. Full-year 2025 revenue was $277.2M, up 16.6 percent.

Why the market is wrong

The ticker screens as coal, so ESG mandates, index funds, and most analysts skip it. A $365M market cap with a 0.44 beta and 2 classes of stock keeps institutions away. The market prices the whole company off the dirtiest label in its filings.

Our variant view rests on the contract structure. A conventional coal miner owns the mine and eats commodity risk. NACCO's unconsolidated mines pass fuel cost through to the utility and pay NACCO a fee per ton. That makes the core segment closer to a service business than a commodity producer. Royalty and mining service peers trade at 1.5x to 2.5x book. NACCO has historically traded near 1x book and now sits at 0.83x, even as Q1 earnings grew 80 percent and management returns cash through a raised dividend and an active buyback.

Valuation

At $48.40 the stock trades at 0.83x book value of $57.97 per share. Trailing twelve-month EPS is $2.86, which puts the trailing P/E at 16.9x. Annualizing Q1 2026 EPS of $1.17 implies roughly 10.3x forward earnings if the run rate holds.

One correction matters here. The original screen claimed roughly 8x earnings. Verification refuted that number: 2025 net income fell 48 percent to $17.6M, so the true trailing multiple is 16.9x. The thesis survives on the annualized Q1 run rate and the book value discount, and we underwrite it on those figures.

A return to 1x book, where NACCO has traded historically, puts the stock near $58, about 20 percent upside before dividends. Book value itself grows as retained earnings and sub-book buybacks compound. We think fair value sits in the $55 to $62 range, and the hard asset backing limits the downside.

Risks

The core risk is duration. The lignite fee contracts serve a handful of coal-fired power plants, and 1 plant closure can erase a large fee stream overnight. The long-run decline of coal generation caps how long the core segment earns anything. This is why NC ranked last of our 14 picks despite passing verification: the cash flows are real, and they are finite.

Verification also flagged softness under the headline growth. Q1 2026 revenue fell 4 percent year over year even as EPS rose, and 2025 net income dropped 48 percent from 2024. Some analysts flag margin compression. If the Q1 earnings pace fades back toward the trailing $2.86, the stock trades at 16.9x a shrinking business and the thesis fails. Dual-class control by the founding family also removes any takeover path and lets the discount persist.

Catalysts

Q2 2026 earnings in early August will show whether the $1.17 quarterly run rate holds; 2 more quarters near that level would force screens to reprice the forward multiple. The $20M buyback runs through December 31, 2027 and retires roughly 5 percent of the market cap at current prices, all below book. North American Mining growth, including the Army Corps dragline project, keeps shifting the mix away from lignite. Each dividend raise, most recently 4 percent in May 2026 to $1.05 annualized, signals board confidence in the fee streams.
