Genpact: the market prices AI as a killer. We think it pays the bills.

AI Investment Team · Initiated July 2026 · NYSE: G

Price
~$29.16 (July 2, 2026)
Market cap
~$4.9B (169.5M shares)
Valuation
~7x forward P/E on guided $4.10 FY2026 EPS
52-week range
$27.37 – $48.64
Recommendation
Long

Thesis

Genpact trades at roughly 7x forward earnings because investors believe agentic AI will destroy business process outsourcing. The stock fell about 40% this year on that fear and now sits near its 52-week low of $27.37. We think the market has the direction of the AI effect wrong for this specific company.

Genpact's Advanced Technology Solutions segment grew 24% year over year in Q1 2026 and now makes up 27% of revenue. Management reiterated 20%+ ATS growth for Q2. In addition to this, the company ships its own agentic AI products, including an agentic deductions tool for consumer goods clients that launched in July 2026. Total revenue still grew 6.7% in Q1 and EPS of $0.98 beat estimates. A company priced for terminal decline is growing, buying back stock, and guiding higher.

At $29, the market pays nothing for the AI segment succeeding. If ATS keeps compounding at 20% while the core holds flat, the multiple re-rates. If the core erodes faster than ATS scales, we own it at 7x earnings with net debt under 1x EBITDA. That asymmetry is the trade.

Business

Genpact runs business operations for large companies. It handles finance and accounting, supply chain, procurement, insurance claims, and customer service work that clients outsource. The company started as a GE unit and spun out in 2007. It employs teams that process transactions and manage workflows for Fortune 500 clients on multi-year contracts.

Revenue splits two ways. Traditional outsourcing work, mostly billed per employee, makes up around 73% of revenue and grows slowly. Advanced Technology Solutions, the newer 27%, sells data engineering, analytics, and AI-driven automation, including Genpact's own agentic products. Management calls this pivot "Genpact Next." Q1 2026 revenue was $1.3B, up 6.7%, and Q2 guidance calls for $1.324B to $1.336B.

Why the market is wrong

The market treats every BPO stock as roadkill for AI agents. Citi cut its price target to $35 and Mizuho flagged the 73% traditional-revenue exposure. The stock de-rated 40% in six months on a narrative, not on results. Every reported number went the other way. Revenue grew. EPS beat. The AI segment accelerated. Guidance held.

Our variant view is simple. Genpact knows its clients' back-office processes better than anyone, because it runs them. That position lets it sell AI automation into workflows it already controls, converting labor revenue into higher-margin technology revenue. The 24% ATS growth is evidence the conversion works. The market sees AI as pure threat. We see a company that captures part of the disruption it faces, priced as if it captures none.

This also gives us a clean disconfirming test. If ATS growth breaks below 20% for two straight quarters, the thesis is wrong and we exit. Few cheap stocks offer a signal that clear.

Valuation

Management guides FY2026 adjusted diluted EPS to about $4.10 with net revenue growth of 7%+. At $29.16 that is roughly a 7.1x forward P/E. It touched 6.7x at the July 1 low of $27.50. AI-services peers like EXL Service trade far higher, and even a stagnant-services multiple of 10x to 12x puts the stock at $41 to $49 on current guidance. Consensus 12-month targets sit around $42, about 44% above the price, and even the freshly cut targets (Citi $35, Baird $45) sit above it.

The balance sheet supports the case. Genpact holds about $930M in cash against $1.76B of debt, under 1x EBITDA net. Buybacks of roughly $283M shrank the share count 2.4% year over year. At 7x earnings, buybacks retire around 14% earnings yield worth of stock. We do not need multiple expansion to make money here. Guided earnings plus shrinking share count does most of the work, and any re-rating toward 10x is upside.

Risks

The bear case is real and we take it seriously. Around 73% of revenue is traditional, largely FTE-billed outsourcing, and Mizuho confirms that core is decelerating. If clients use AI to cut headcount-based contracts faster than ATS scales, total revenue shrinks and the stock stays cheap for a reason. Pricing deflation on renewals is the mechanism to watch, not client losses.

Second, agentic AI products are new. Pilots must convert to production-scale contracts, and the July 2026 deductions launch is one product, not a proven franchise. Third, this is a falling knife by price action, down 40% year to date, and sentiment can push it lower before fundamentals matter. Because of this, we size the position for volatility and hold our stop on the ATS growth signal, not on price.

Catalysts

Q2 2026 earnings, due early August, is the near-term test. Guidance calls for $1.324B to $1.336B in revenue, EPS of $0.96 to $0.97, and 20%+ ATS growth. A confirmed beat against a stock priced for decline forces a repricing. Client wins for the agentic deductions product launched in July would show the AI franchise converts pilots into contracts. Continued buybacks against a 169.5M share count add a steady mechanical bid. Also, any sell-side upgrade cycle off the $42 consensus target would help, since coverage turned cautious right at the low.
