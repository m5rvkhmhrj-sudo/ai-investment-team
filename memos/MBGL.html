Mobility Global: a 2-day-old spin-off nobody has to own

Thesis

S&P Global spun off Mobility Global on July 1, 2026, distributing one MBGL share for each SPGI share. Nobody who received the stock chose to own it. SPGI holders bought a large-cap index and ratings franchise, and index funds that track SPGI cannot hold a $6.3 billion auto-data company. The mechanical selling showed up on day one. MBGL closed at $20.26, down around 4.4 percent, while the parent rose 4.5 to 6 percent.

What the sellers are dumping is CARFAX plus Polk, a recurring-revenue auto-data franchise that produced $1.75 billion of 2025 revenue, $724 million of LTM adjusted EBITDA, and $461 million of free cash flow. Net leverage sits at only 2.6x. At $21.19 the stock trades at around 11 to 12x EV/EBITDA. Premium data-analytics peers trade at 14 to 18x.

Forced selling after a mega-cap parent distribution typically clears within weeks. Coverage is thin, with 3 analysts plus a fresh Baird initiation. We think the discount closes as the shareholder base turns over and coverage builds. The memo does not need the $37 consensus target to work. Multiple convergence toward the low end of the peer range gets us there.

Business

Mobility Global sells data and software to the auto industry. Its brands are CARFAX, Polk Automotive Solutions, automotiveMastermind, and Market Scan. CARFAX is the crown jewel. It holds the dominant database of vehicle history in North America. Dealers pay for it to price and certify used cars, and consumers know the brand well enough that used-car listings without a CARFAX report look suspect.

Polk supplies registration and vehicle-in-operation data to automakers, insurers, and marketers. automotiveMastermind sells predictive sales software to dealers. Market Scan powers payment and lease calculations. Revenue is mostly subscription and repeat-use data licensing, which is why the company converts EBITDA to cash at a high rate: $461 million of FCF on $724 million of adjusted EBITDA.

The company carries $2 billion of spin-related debt in three tranches priced between 5.05 and 6.05 percent, costing around $111 million of annual interest. FCF covers that more than 4 times over.

Why the market is wrong

This is analyst neglect in its purest form. The stock is 2 days old. Only 3 sell-side analysts publish estimates. There is no trading history, no standalone earnings call, and no natural buyer base yet. Meanwhile the sellers are not making a judgment about value. Index funds and large-cap mandates sell because their rules force them to.

Inside S&P Global, Mobility was a small segment buried under Ratings and Indices. Nobody built a model for it. As a standalone filer it becomes legible: a comprehensible data franchise with 2.6x leverage and strong cash conversion. That legibility usually arrives with coverage initiations and inclusion in mid-cap indices, both of which bring buyers who chose the stock.

We also flag what the bull case gets wrong. The widely quoted $37 average target rests on 3 analysts, all at exactly $37, and Baird just initiated at Neutral with a $23 target. We treat the consensus as weak evidence and underwrite the multiple instead.

Valuation

At $21.19 the enterprise value is roughly $8.1 billion against $724 million of LTM adjusted EBITDA, or about 11 to 12x. Data and analytics companies with recurring revenue and strong brands trade at 14 to 18x. We do not need the high end. At 14x EBITDA, the low end of the peer range, the equity is worth around $27 to 28 per share, roughly 30 percent above today. At 16x it is worth low-to-mid $30s.

The FCF check supports this. $461 million of free cash flow on a $6.27 billion market cap is a 7.4 percent FCF yield for a subscription data business. Peers with this profile yield 4 to 5 percent. We are paying a cyclical-industrial price for a data-franchise cash flow stream.

The real dispersion in sell-side targets is $23 to $37. Even the bearish end of that range, Baird's $23, sits above the current price.

Risks

The strongest bear case is leverage into a cyclical end market. MBGL sells to auto dealers and automakers. If new-car and used-car volumes weaken, dealer software and marketing budgets contract, and the $2 billion of debt amplifies the equity downside. A recession that cuts EBITDA 20 percent would push leverage above 3x and compress the multiple at the same time.

Selling pressure can also persist longer than expected. SPGI has around 300 million shares outstanding, so the entire MBGL float landed in hands that mostly do not want it. Weeks is the typical clearing time, but there is no guarantee.

Two honesty items from our verification pass. First, calling CARFAX a monopoly is thesis framing, not a verified fact. It is dominant, but AutoCheck and OEM data programs compete. Second, the 3-analyst consensus is thin and clustered, and Baird's Neutral $23 initiation shows a credible analyst sees limited upside. If Baird is right about standalone margins, the multiple rerating stalls.

Catalysts

Forced-selling exhaustion over July and August 2026 as index funds finish distributing the position. First standalone earnings report, expected in August 2026, which gives the market its first clean look at segment margins and guidance. Additional coverage initiations following Baird, since spin-offs typically pick up 5 to 10 analysts within two quarters. Potential mid-cap index inclusion, which brings mechanical buyers to replace the mechanical sellers. Any capital-return announcement, since $461 million of FCF against $111 million of interest leaves room for buybacks or a dividend.
