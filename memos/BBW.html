Build-A-Bear Workshop: buying back stock at the bottom while the market prices a Google panic

Recommendation
Long

Thesis

Build-A-Bear trades at $31.50, near its 52-week low of $30.26 and 58% below its September 2025 high of $75.85. The selloff has one driver. Google's AI search changes cut the company's e-commerce traffic 26.1% in the first quarter, and the market decided the whole business is broken. We think it priced a channel problem as a company problem.

Management disagrees with the market and has said so with cash. The company repurchased about $53M of stock under its $100M authorization, retiring roughly 650,000 shares, about 5% of the company, in the trailing 12 months. It kept buying through May 27, 2026, adding 89,966 shares for $3.3M after the quarter closed. That is a board and management team buying their own shares at trough prices while the stock makes new lows.

Meanwhile the business still earns money. Q1 diluted EPS came in at $1.45, up from $1.17 a year earlier, even with revenue down 2.4%. Guidance calls for $530-550M of revenue and $72-78M of pre-tax income this year. At roughly 6-7x forward earnings with a 2.9% dividend and an active buyback, we do not need the panic to reverse. We need the company to stay roughly this profitable.

Business

Build-A-Bear sells stuffed animals that customers assemble in the store. A child picks a shell, stuffs it, dresses it, and names it. The experience is the product, which is why the concept has survived two decades of mall decline. The company runs around 500 locations worldwide, a mix of corporate stores, partner-operated stores, and franchises.

Revenue comes from three channels. Retail stores produce most of it. E-commerce, the channel Google hurt, produces roughly a quarter. The commercial and franchise segment licenses the brand and wholesales product to partners, and it grew 34% last quarter. A new partnership put Build-A-Bear product in more than 1,500 Walmart stores starting spring 2026. The model throws off cash because inventory is cheap plush and the stores are small.

Why the market is wrong

The market read one bad channel as a secular verdict on the company. E-commerce fell 26.1% because Google's AI answers now intercept search traffic before it reaches retail sites. That is real and it hit hard. However, it touches maybe a quarter of revenue. Domestic store traffic fell only 7%, gross margin expanded 700 basis points to 63.8%, and the commercial segment grew 34%. Total revenue fell just 2.4%. A structurally broken retailer does not print those numbers.

Coverage is thin, which lets the mispricing sit. Four analysts follow the stock, all rating it Strong Buy, with an average target of $61.25, nearly double the current price. Small-cap consumer names with a scary headline and no sponsorship stay cheap until something forces a repricing. Here the company itself is the forcing function. Every quarter of buybacks at $31 shrinks the float and raises EPS on flat earnings. Management retired 8.63% of the company in about a year and has $47M of authorization left, more than 12% of the current market cap, with the program running through September 2028.

Valuation

We anchor on adjusted numbers, not the headline. Q1's $1.45 EPS includes a $7M one-time tariff refund; adjusted EPS was $1.03. Full-year guidance of $72-78M pre-tax income also includes $13M of tariff refunds. Stripping that out and taxing normally, we get around $4.50-5.00 of EPS, which puts the stock at roughly 6-7x forward earnings. Trailing P/E is 7.4x on $4.26 of TTM EPS.

A simple case: hold earnings flat at $4.50 and let the buyback run. Retiring 5% of shares a year adds about 5% to EPS with no operating growth. At a modest 10x multiple on $4.50, the stock is worth $45, about 43% above today, before dividends. If the Walmart rollout and 20%+ commercial growth offset e-commerce decline and earnings grow at all, the sell-side target near $61 stops looking aggressive. The 2.9% dividend pays us to wait. We think fair value sits in the $45-55 range and the downside is cushioned by the buyback bid underneath the stock.

Risks

The bear case is that AI search disruption is secular, not cyclical. If Google's AI answers permanently reroute discovery away from retail sites, and mall traffic keeps eroding on top of it, then this year's guidance is the peak and the buyback is burning cash into a melting business. E-commerce down 26% in one quarter is not a normal fluctuation. This is the strongest argument against us and we cannot fully refute it yet. Two more quarters of traffic data will tell us.

Earnings quality needs watching. The $7M tariff refund added roughly 560 of the 700 basis points of Q1 gross margin gain, and $13M of refunds sit inside full-year guidance. The underlying run-rate is weaker than headlines suggest. There is also a fresh CEO transition: Chris Hurt replaced longtime CEO Sharon Price John on June 11, 2026. The succession was planned, and John drove the last five years of margin expansion, so execution risk under new leadership is real. Finally, this is a $385M small cap with a 12M share float. It trades thin and it will be volatile.

Catalysts

Q2 FY2026 earnings, expected late August 2026, give the first clean read on whether e-commerce declines are stabilizing and how the Walmart rollout is tracking. Continued buyback disclosures matter too: $47M remains on the authorization, and each 10-Q shows whether management kept buying at these prices. The Walmart partnership in 1,500+ stores ramps through the 2026 holiday season, and holiday commercial orders show up in the October and January reports. Any of the four covering analysts refreshing targets after a stabilization quarter could move a stock this small on its own.
