Thesis

CONMED trades near $33.83, within 2 percent of its 52-week low, after falling around 41 percent in a year. Management guides 2026 adjusted EPS to $4.30 to $4.45, so the stock costs about 7.7x forward adjusted earnings. Surgical device peers such as Stryker, Teleflex, and Integra trade at 13x to 25x forward. CONMED itself traded above 20x forward for most of the last decade.

We think the market punishes a growing, margin-recovering business as if it were shrinking. Management raised 2026 organic constant-currency growth guidance to 5.0 to 6.5 percent on the Q1 call. Q1 2026 gross margin improved to 57.9 percent from 55.3 percent, and operating margin hit 8.0 percent against 5.0 percent a year earlier. The margin problems from 2024 and 2025 are fading in the reported numbers.

The discount exists because CONMED keeps hurting itself. Supply and warehouse disruptions, a heavy acquisition debt load, and now a CFO change give investors reasons to wait. We think those problems are fixable and temporary while the product franchises are durable. At this price a buyer also gets the best mid-cap takeout optionality in our pool for free.

Business

CONMED sells orthopedic and general-surgery devices from Largo, Florida. A large share of revenue comes from single-use products that hospitals reorder with every procedure, which makes sales recurring rather than lumpy. The portfolio holds strong niche positions: AirSeal in insufflation for laparoscopic and robotic surgery, Buffalo Filter in surgical smoke evacuation, and a broad arthroscopy line in sports medicine.

Q1 2026 sales were $317.0M, down 1.3 percent year over year. That decline is deliberate. CONMED exited a GI product line that removed $15.5M from the quarter, and the rest of the business grew. GAAP EPS came in at $0.45, more than double the prior year, and adjusted EPS was $0.89. The Biorez and In2Bones acquisitions added growth assets in sports medicine and extremities but also left meaningful debt on the balance sheet.

Why the market is wrong

The market treats CONMED's execution history as a permanent trait. BofA downgraded the stock to Underperform on June 29, 2026 on growth concerns, and shares fell about 4.9 percent. A new CFO, John Gallagher from Certara and BD, starts July 15, 2026. Both events sound like reasons to sell. We read them as reasons the discount exists, and both are consistent with a company fixing itself rather than one in decline.

Our variant view rests on the reported numbers. Guidance went up, margins expanded sharply, and the headline sales decline traces entirely to a planned product exit. In June 2026 the company repurchased about $645.2M of its 2.25 percent convertible notes for roughly $637.2M in cash, funded by a new $450M senior secured term loan due 2030. That swap adds at least $0.10 per share of interest expense, and we count that cost. In exchange it removes the conversion dilution overhang and clears the 2027 maturity. A business guiding to 5 to 6.5 percent organic growth with expanding margins does not deserve a multiple reserved for melting ice cubes. If public investors keep refusing to pay for AirSeal and Buffalo Filter, a strategic acquirer at $1.05B of market cap eventually will.

Valuation

At $33.83 the stock trades at roughly 7.7x the $4.30 to $4.45 adjusted EPS guidance that management reaffirmed on April 30, 2026. Our original work cited $32 to $33 and 7.5x; the stock traded $32.11 to $32.92 in late June, so those figures were slightly stale but directionally right. We also cited about 18x trailing GAAP earnings. Verification could not confirm that exact figure from primary filings, though nothing contradicted it, so we anchor on the forward adjusted multiple.

The re-rate math needs no heroics. At 12x forward adjusted EPS, still the bottom of the peer range, the stock is worth around $52, about 55 percent above today. A return to the 52-week high of $61.08 requires 14x. If the multiple never moves, the earnings themselves compound: guidance implies the company earns back around 13 percent of its market cap in adjusted earnings each year. Debt is the main haircut. The new term loan and acquisition borrowings mean equity holders carry leverage, so we size the position for that.

Risks

Execution risk kills this thesis, and CONMED has stumbled repeatedly. Past supply-chain and warehouse disruptions were self-inflicted, and another operational miss would keep the discount in place indefinitely. The CFO transition raises the odds of a reset quarter; Todd Garner stays in an advisory role only through November 2, 2026. Debt from Biorez and In2Bones plus the new $450M secured term loan reduces the margin for error, and the convert refinancing added at least $0.10 per share of annual interest expense.

Two honest caveats from verification. First, our valuation multiples rest on aggregator price quotes rather than primary filings, which is why the verification team assigned medium rather than high confidence. Second, the BofA Underperform call argues growth will disappoint; if organic growth lands below the 5.0 percent low end of guidance, the cheap multiple is a trap rather than an opportunity. The adjusted EPS figure also excludes real costs, so GAAP earnings run well below the headline number we underwrite.

Catalysts

The Q2 2026 earnings call on July 29, 2026 is the near-term test. Management guided Q2 adjusted EPS to $1.09 to $1.14, and hitting that range with the raised 5.0 to 6.5 percent organic growth outlook intact starts rebuilding credibility. John Gallagher's arrival as CFO on July 15, 2026 gives the story a fresh operator, and his first full quarter could bring conservative rebased targets that the company then beats. The June 2026 convert repurchase already removed the dilution overhang, so every dollar of earnings growth now accrues to the current share count. Further out, continued margin recovery toward historical levels or an approach from a strategic acquirer closes the gap faster than the market expects.
