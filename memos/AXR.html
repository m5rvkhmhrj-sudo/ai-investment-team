Thesis

AMREP owns thousands of acres in Rio Rancho, New Mexico, carried on the books at historical cost from the 1960s and 1970s. The stock trades near stated book value, and stated book badly understates what the land is worth today. You get a real estate portfolio marked at 50-year-old cost, plus a profitable homebuilding operation, for roughly the accounting value of both.

This is a nano-cap net-asset story where the assets are monetizing, not rotting. Nine-month FY2026 net income ran $9.0M, or $1.68 per diluted share, against a $130.8M market cap. The balance sheet holds $50.0M of cash against $21K of notes payable, so the company is essentially debt-free. A long record of converting land into cash means we get paid while the discount closes.

Our verification pass tried to refute the thesis and failed on every checkable claim. Results improved after the thesis was formed. Q3 FY2026 net income was $3.1M against $0.7M a year earlier.

Business

AMREP Corporation traces to the original development of Rio Rancho, now the third-largest city in New Mexico and a suburb of Albuquerque. The company operates 2 segments. Land development entitles and sells residential and commercial lots in Rio Rancho, mostly to homebuilders. Homebuilding, run through its Amreston brand, builds and sells homes on that same land.

The model is simple. Land acquired decades ago sits on the balance sheet at original cost. Each year the company develops a slice, sells lots or finished homes at current market prices, and books gains that accounting cost never anticipated. Nine-month FY2026 revenue was $41.8M. Shareholders' equity stood at $139.4M at January 31, 2026, around $26.30 per share.

Why the market is wrong

The market prices AMREP off its stated book, and stated book is the wrong yardstick. GAAP carries the Rio Rancho acreage at 1960s-era cost. Every lot sale proves the gap: the company keeps selling land above carrying value, which is why a business trading below book still earns a 10.6x P/E. Homebuilder peers trade at 1.2x to 2x book on inventory marked at recent cost. AMREP trades at 0.94x book on land marked at ancient cost, so the true discount to net asset value is far larger than the headline multiple suggests.

Why does the gap exist? A ~5.3M share float, no analyst coverage, and no index sponsorship. Nobody is paid to notice a $131M company in Albuquerque exurbs. That neglect is the opportunity, and it is also the main risk we underwrite below.

One correction to our original work. The initial thesis cited 0.89x book. At the late-June price of $24.65 against $139.4M of equity, the multiple is ~0.94x. Macrotrends showed 0.83x in January 2026, so the stock has traded across that range this year. The correction does not change the argument, because the argument rests on the land marks, not the last decimal of P/B.

Valuation

At $24.65 the stock trades at ~0.94x the $26.30 of stated book value per share and ~10.6x earnings. Nine-month FY2026 EPS of $1.68 annualizes to a low double-digit earnings yield on a debt-free company where cash alone covers 38 percent of the market cap.

We do not attempt a precise land appraisal; nobody outside the company can. Directionally, entitled lots near a growing Albuquerque metro are worth multiples of 1960s cost. A move to just 1.2x stated book, the bottom of the homebuilder peer range, implies roughly $31.50, or 28 percent upside, and still gives no credit to the land markup. If earnings hold near the current pace, book value grows about $1.70 per share a year, so the floor rises while we wait. Downside is protected by $50M of cash and land the company demonstrably sells at a profit.

Risks

The monetization pace depends entirely on Albuquerque-area housing demand. A regional slowdown stalls lot sales, and the earnings stream is already lumpy. Q2 FY2026 net income fell to $1.2M from $4.0M the year before on lower land-sale revenue, so single quarters can look ugly without meaning much.

The thin float cuts both ways. With ~5.3M shares, no coverage, and low volume, the discount can persist for years, and exiting a position of any size takes patience. There is no dividend and no buyback commitment to force the gap closed. FY2026 full-year results, for the year ended April 30, 2026, had not been published as of early July, so the annual numbers could disappoint against the strong nine-month run. Finally, our NAV claim rests on directional logic about old land marks, not an independent appraisal. If Rio Rancho land values fall materially, the hidden cushion shrinks with them.

Catalysts

FY2026 annual results, covering the year ended April 30, 2026, should arrive in July 2026 and will show whether the $1.68 nine-month EPS pace held. Continued quarterly land and home sales convert unmarked acreage into cash and reported book value, which forces the market to reprice the balance sheet one filing at a time. Population growth in Rio Rancho and the broader Albuquerque metro supports lot demand. At this size, any coverage initiation, index inclusion, or strategic interest in the land bank would rerate the stock quickly. Absent all of that, retained earnings compound book value while we hold.
