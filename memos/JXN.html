Thesis

Jackson Financial trades at $104.47 against $138.17 of common equity per diluted share and $22.67 of 2025 adjusted operating EPS. That is 4.6x earnings and 0.76x book. We think the market misreads an annuity writer that returns most of its earnings to shareholders as an uninvestable black box.

The engine here is capital return. Adjusted operating EPS grew from $18.79 in 2024 to $22.67 in 2025, and much of that growth came from a shrinking share count. Management targets $900M to $1.1B of capital return in 2026, around 13 percent of the market cap at the midpoint. On June 30, 2026 the company signed a new $1.25B unsecured revolver, replacing a $1.0B facility, which extends its flexibility to keep buying.

Every number in this memo survived adversarial verification against company filings at high confidence. We do not need the business to grow. We need it to keep converting statutory cash flow into a smaller share count while the stock sits below book value.

Business

Jackson is one of the largest sellers of retail annuities in the United States. It spun off from Prudential plc in 2021 and runs its business from Lansing, Michigan. Customers, mostly retirees and near-retirees, hand Jackson a lump sum in exchange for guaranteed or market-linked income. Jackson earns fees on the assets, spread on the general account, and it hedges the equity-market guarantees embedded in its variable annuity book.

The product mix has shifted toward registered index-linked annuities (RILAs), which carry lighter guarantees and less tail risk than legacy variable annuities. Retail annuity sales ran $5.3B in Q1 2026, up 31 percent year over year. The statutory side of the house is healthy: risk-based capital sits above the company's 425 percent minimum, which is what funds the dividend and buyback program.

Why the market is wrong

Annuity accounting is genuinely ugly. Hedging gains and losses flow through GAAP earnings, so Jackson posted a $435M GAAP net loss in Q1 2026 even while adjusted operating earnings came in at $5.15 per share. Trailing GAAP EPS is negative, so the stock shows no P/E on most screens. Quantitative screens skip it, and analyst coverage rates it a Hold with $113 to $123 targets. This is the neglect we look for.

Our variant view is simple. The economic earnings of this business are the adjusted operating earnings plus the statutory cash the subsidiaries dividend up, and both are strong. The GAAP noise is the hedge doing its job, moving opposite the liabilities it protects. A buyer at 0.76x book who watches share count instead of quarterly GAAP prints gets paid twice: once from the earnings yield above 20 percent, and again from every share retired below book value, which mechanically raises book value per share for everyone who stays.

Valuation

At $104.47 the stock trades at 4.6x 2025 adjusted operating EPS of $22.67. Common equity per diluted share was $138.17 at December 31, 2025, and adjusted book value was $155.78. We anchor on the lower figure.

We do not need a heroic multiple. A re-rate to 6x adjusted operating earnings, still a discount to annuity peers, puts the stock near $136, roughly 30 percent upside before capital return. Holding the multiple flat, the buyback alone does the work: retiring shares at 0.76x book compounds book value per share by high single digits a year on top of the earnings yield. Closing the gap to stated book value of $138 implies similar upside. We think fair value sits in the $130 to $140 range and the current price pays us to wait.

One honest caveat on run rate. Q1 2026 adjusted operating EPS was $5.15, or $5.94 excluding notable items, which annualizes near the 2025 figure rather than above it. Our math assumes flat earnings, not growth.

Risks

The bear case is real and market-linked. Variable annuity hedging results are opaque, and a sharp equity drawdown can hit statutory capital, push RBC toward the 425 percent floor, and pause the buyback. The Q1 2026 GAAP loss of $435M shows how violently reported results can swing. If capital return stops, the discount to book can persist indefinitely, and this becomes a value trap.

The buyback pace has already moderated. Of the $257M returned in Q1 2026, only around $29M was repurchases (1.7M shares); the rest was dividends. If management keeps favoring dividends over buybacks with the stock below book, the per-share compounding we underwrite slows. Legacy variable annuity guarantees also carry long-tail policyholder behavior risk that outsiders, including us, cannot fully model. Sustained low rates or a credit cycle in the general account portfolio would pressure spread income.

Catalysts

Q2 2026 earnings in early August should show whether buybacks reaccelerate against the $900M to $1.1B full-year target; the new $1.25B revolver signed June 30, 2026 gives management no funding excuse. Continued RILA sales growth (Q1 ran +31 percent) shifts the book toward products the market prices more generously. Each quarter of capital return at these prices shrinks the float and raises book value per share, so time itself works for the position. A recovery toward the 52-week high of $123.61 requires no multiple expansion beyond what analysts' own $113 to $123 targets already imply.
