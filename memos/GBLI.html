Thesis

Global Indemnity trades at $25.05 against $47.92 of book value per share at March 31, 2026. That is 0.52x book for an insurer with no debt, $1.4B of cash and investments, and $273M of discretionary capital against a $366M market cap. The market pays 52 cents for a dollar of mostly investment-grade bonds.

This is the cleanest hard-asset discount we found in our screen. Book value here consists of markable securities. The company carries no goodwill story and no leverage. Underwriting turned the corner in Q1 2026 with a 95.1 percent combined ratio, up from 111.7 percent a year earlier. Operating income ran $8.3M in the quarter, or $0.57 per share.

Chairman Saul Fox controls the company through Fox Paine and has explored strategic alternatives, including hiring Insurance Advisory Partners and adding M&A Services CEO Jason Murgio to the board in June 2025. We collect a 5.6 percent dividend yield while we wait for him to act. Every core fact in this memo survived adversarial verification against July 2026 data at high confidence.

Business

Global Indemnity writes specialty property and casualty insurance in the excess and surplus lines market. E&S carriers insure risks that standard admitted insurers decline: small commercial accounts, vacant properties, collectibles, and other niche exposures. The company operates through its Penn-America segment and is building out its Katalyx distribution and Belmont technology platforms.

The investment portfolio is conservative. Shareholders' equity sits near $700M to $704M against $1.4B of cash and investments, mostly fixed maturities that management has shifted toward Treasuries. Net investment income ran $12.2M in Q1 2026, down from $14.8M a year earlier as the portfolio de-risked. Net earned premiums were $98.4M in the quarter. Fox Paine holds voting control, which makes this a controlled company under NYSE rules.

Why the market is wrong

Screens punish GBLI for its history. The company posted a 111.7 percent combined ratio in the prior-year quarter and earns a mid-single-digit ROE, so quantitative value screens see a sub-par underwriter and move on. Coverage is thin. The controlled-company structure scares off institutions that want a say in governance.

Our variant view rests on 3 points. First, the discount is too wide for the asset quality. Specialty P&C peers like Kinsale, RLI, and Palomar trade at 2x to 5x book. Even sleepy small P&C names trade near 1x. A bond portfolio with no debt should not clear at 0.52x. Second, underwriting has already inflected, and the market has not repriced the 95.1 percent combined ratio. Third, the control block cuts both ways. Fox owns the outcome. He hired bankers, added an M&A executive to the board, and holds $273M of discretionary capital he can deploy into buybacks, a special dividend, or a sale. Any of those closes the gap.

Valuation

Book value per share was $47.92 at March 31, 2026, down from $48.96 at December 31, 2025. The slip came partly from investment losses and the $0.35 quarterly dividend, so we underwrite the current number, not the peak. At $25.05 the stock trades at 0.52x that figure. Our original screen cited a $340M market cap; the correct figure is $366M at 14.61M shares, which we use here. The 0.52x multiple holds either way.

We do not need peer multiples to work. A move to 0.75x book puts the stock near $36, about 43 percent upside. Full book value is $47.92, and the one analyst target sits at $48, roughly 92 percent upside. A sale or large buyback funded from the $273M of discretionary capital would realize value faster than either path. Meanwhile the $1.40 annual dividend pays 5.6 percent at cost. Downside protection is the point: the price already discounts a 48 percent haircut to a bond portfolio.

Risks

The discount has a reason. A mid-single-digit ROE below the cost of equity justifies trading under book, and if underwriting slips back toward the 111.7 percent combined ratio of early 2025, the market will keep it there. Book value can also erode; it fell about $1 per share in Q1 2026 on investment losses and dividends. Net investment income is declining as the portfolio shifts to Treasuries.

The bigger risk is timing. Minority holders wait on Saul Fox's timetable, and he owes them nothing. On the Q1 2026 call, management emphasized Katalyx and Belmont growth spending and tech transformation over buybacks or a near-term sale. The strategic-alternatives process could sit dormant for years. If the catalyst never fires, this becomes a 5.6 percent yield with dead money attached, and at 14.61M shares the float is thin enough that exiting a position takes patience. Our verification flagged the soft catalyst timing as the weakest link in the thesis, and we agree.

Catalysts

Q2 2026 earnings in early August should confirm whether the 95.1 percent combined ratio holds; a second clean quarter forces the market to reprice the underwriting turn. Any deployment of the $273M discretionary capital pile, buyback, special dividend, or acquisition, changes the math immediately. The strategic process remains live in the background: Insurance Advisory Partners is engaged and Jason Murgio joined the board in June 2025, so a sale announcement can arrive without warning. The $0.35 quarterly dividend pays us about 5.6 percent while we wait, and the stock sits within 3 percent of its 52-week low of $24.28, so entry timing favors us.
