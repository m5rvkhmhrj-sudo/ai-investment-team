Thesis

Taylor Devices makes seismic dampers and shock isolation systems for bridges, skyscrapers, and missile launch platforms. It holds a near-monopoly position in its niche, carries $41.7M in cash with zero debt, and no sell-side analyst covers it. The company is posting record results: 9-month FY26 sales of $32.7M and net income of $6.7M, both records.

Trailing 12-month net income runs about $10.4M, or roughly $3.28 per share. At $57 the stock trades near 17x trailing earnings and about 13x once you strip out the cash pile, which covers more than 20 percent of the market cap. Our original screen pegged the multiple at 20 to 22x. Verification against filings showed the real number is cheaper, and the stock's 21 percent drop after the March 31 Q3 report handed us an entry well below the thesis price.

Gross margins have re-rated above 40 percent under new management's pricing discipline. Aerospace and defense demand, led by missile launch dampers, gives a growth leg the market still prices as a sleepy legacy industrial. We get downside protection from the balance sheet and upside from structurally higher earnings power.

Business

Taylor Devices operates out of North Tonawanda, New York, and has built shock and vibration control products for decades. Its dampers protect buildings and bridges in earthquake zones, and its isolation systems ride on aerospace and defense platforms. Projects are engineered to order, so bookings arrive lumpy and revenue follows the firm order backlog.

Recent quarters show the model working. Q2 FY26 delivered record sales of $11.6M with $2.01M of net income ($0.64 EPS). Q3 FY26 added $11.17M of sales and $2.50M of net income ($0.79 EPS). Full-year FY25 (ended May 2025) came in at $46.3M of sales with a 20.3 percent net margin. In July 2025 the company signed an exclusive UK and Europe distribution agreement with Force Development Services, opening a channel it never had before.

Why the market is wrong

Nobody looks at this stock. Zero analysts cover it, the float is tiny at 3.17M shares, and quantitative screens see a micro-cap industrial with lumpy revenue. The market treats today's 40 percent-plus gross margins and 20 percent net margins as a cyclical peak. We think they reflect a durable pricing reset in a niche where Taylor faces almost no direct competition.

The March selloff proves the neglect. The stock fell about 21 percent on the Q3 release because firm backlog slid from $27.1M to $20.8M, even though the quarter itself set records and management tied the backlog dip to a 6-plus week US government shutdown. Sellers punished a timing issue as if it were lost demand. Historically TAYD traded at 10 to 15x on far lower margins. Today's ~13x ex-cash multiple sits on structurally higher earnings power, while niche defense component peers fetch 25 to 30x.

Valuation

Trailing 12-month EPS runs about $3.28 (FY25 net income of $9.41M, minus the prior 9-month $5.73M, plus the current 9-month $6.70M, on 3.17M shares). At $57 that is roughly 17x trailing earnings. Cash and short-term investments of $41.7M, about $13 per share, take the ex-cash multiple to roughly 13x. Total liabilities sit at just $5.3M against $70.1M of equity.

We do not need heroic assumptions. Hold earnings flat and re-rate to 18x ex-cash, still below defense peers, and the stock reaches the mid $70s, around 30 percent upside. Any recovery in bookings once government funding normalizes adds to that. The cash and debt-free balance sheet cushion the downside: even at the old 10 to 15x range on trailing earnings, the stock holds most of its current value.

Risks

Backlog is the thesis killer. Firm backlog fell from $27.1M to $20.8M during FY26, and management blamed government shutdown delays. If the slide continues into FY27, revenue follows, because project-based bookings feed revenue directly. The market already showed how it reacts: a 21 percent single-day drop on the Q3 report. A second weak backlog print would likely repeat that.

Our verification pass also flagged what the original screen got wrong. The first thesis draft cited a higher share price and a $247M market cap; the correct figures are ~$57 and ~$175–187M. It also overstated the trailing multiple at 20 to 22x versus the real ~17x. Both errors ran in the thesis's favor, but they show how thin the public data is on a name this small. Liquidity is a real cost too: 3.17M shares outstanding means wide spreads, slow exits, and price swings on small volume. Margin durability is unproven across a full cycle, and a few large projects can concentrate revenue risk.

Catalysts

Q4 and full-year FY26 results, due around August 2026 for the fiscal year ended May 31, will show whether backlog rebuilt once the government shutdown effects cleared. A backlog print back above $25M likely reverses much of the March drop. The Force Development Services agreement signed in July 2025 should start producing UK and Europe orders through FY27. Continued missile launch damper demand from defense programs adds bookings independent of construction cycles. With $41.7M of cash and no debt, management can buy back stock or pay a special dividend at any point, and either move would force the market to notice a company no analyst covers.
