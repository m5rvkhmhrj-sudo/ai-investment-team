Doximity: the market priced a monopoly network for permanent decline

AI Investment Team · Initiated July 2026 · NYSE: DOCS

Price
$21.86 (Jul 2, 2026)
Market cap
$4.0B (EV ~$3.1B, net cash $700M+)
Valuation
~9x EV / FY27 EBITDA at entry
52-week range
$17.15 – $76.51
Recommendation
Long

Thesis

Doximity fell 24% in one day after its May 13 report. Guidance for FY27 came in light: revenue of $664 to $676 million against a $699 million consensus, and adjusted EBITDA of $323 to $335 million, down year over year. Jefferies cut its target from $51 to $19. The stock touched $17.15 after trading above $76 within the past year.

We think the selloff confused a budget pause with a broken franchise. Pharma companies froze digital ad spending because of drug pricing policy and turmoil at FDA and HHS. That freeze hits Doximity's near-term numbers. It does not touch the asset itself. Doximity still reaches more than 80% of US physicians, still runs 55.5% EBITDA margins on FY26 numbers, and still converts nearly all of that EBITDA to cash. No competitor has a physician network at any comparable scale.

At roughly 9x EV to FY27 EBITDA, with $700 million plus of net cash and around $1 billion of buyback authorization, the market is pricing this as a business in permanent decline. We think it is pricing a one to two year ad budget cycle. That gap is the trade.

Business

Doximity runs the dominant professional network for US doctors. Physicians use it free for news feeds, secure messaging, telehealth calls, e-signatures, and scheduling tools. The company makes money by selling access to that audience. Pharmaceutical companies pay to put branded drug content in front of prescribers, and health systems pay to recruit and reach clinicians.

The model works because the audience is verified and hard to reach anywhere else. A pharma marketer who wants cardiologists can buy them here with precision that open web advertising cannot match. In FY26, ended March 2026, Doximity generated $644.9 million of revenue, up 13%, with $357.8 million of adjusted EBITDA and $317.5 million of free cash flow. It carries no debt and holds a large net cash position.

Why the market is wrong

The bear case treats the FY27 guide as the new trajectory. We read it as a trough. Pharma HCP marketing budgets froze on policy uncertainty, and Doximity guided to what it can see. Nothing in the quarter showed physicians leaving the platform or advertisers moving to a rival, because no rival network exists at scale.

The verification data flagged two things the bulls gloss over, and we own them. Q4 adjusted EBITDA margin fell to 45.3% from 50.4% a year earlier, and part of that squeeze came from rising AI compute costs, a self-inflicted item rather than a pharma freeze item. The CFO also left in April 2026. Both are real. Neither changes the network position, and management is buying back stock aggressively into the weakness: $432 million repurchased in FY26, with $493 million remaining plus a new $500 million program.

The early evidence supports the variant view. The stock has already started re-rating in early July, and the average target across 22 analysts sits at $24.37 with a Buy consensus. Jefferies at $19 is the outlier, and its own note cites visibility, not competition.

Valuation

Take the low end of FY27 guidance, $323 million of adjusted EBITDA. Against a $3.1 billion enterprise value, that is about 9.6x EV/EBITDA. At the May lows the multiple touched 7 to 8x. FY26 free cash flow of $317.5 million puts the stock near 10x EV/FCF. Forward PE is 15.2 on trough earnings.

We do not need a heroic recovery. If pharma budgets normalize and EBITDA merely returns to the FY26 level of $358 million, a 13x EV/EBITDA multiple, still a discount to what this quality of business has commanded, implies an enterprise value near $4.7 billion. Add back the cash and shrink the share count with the buyback, and we get a value in the low $30s per share, around 40 to 50% above the current price. The net cash pile and the FCF stream limit the downside if the recovery takes longer.

Risks

The strongest bear case is that the pharma digital ad market stays structurally soft. If drug pricing policy permanently restricts HCP and DTC marketing, FY27 is not a trough, and the guide keeps stepping down. That would break the thesis.

Second, AI tools could erode the workflow engagement that creates Doximity's ad inventory. If physicians get news, references, and messaging from AI assistants instead of the app, the audience thins out. Related to this, Doximity's own AI spending is already compressing margins: the Q4 margin drop to 45.3% was partly compute cost, not just revenue mix.

Third, execution risk is elevated. The CFO departed in April 2026, right before the guidance reset. We also note the stock remains volatile; a $17 to $76 52-week range means the market has no firm view on what this business is worth.

Catalysts

Q1 FY27 earnings on August 6, 2026 is the first test of whether guidance was set conservatively. Any sign that pharma budgets are unfreezing, or a guide raise, moves the stock. The buyback is a standing catalyst: roughly $1 billion of authorization against a $4 billion market cap retires shares fast at these prices. A permanent CFO hire would remove an overhang. Further out, clarity on drug pricing policy through late 2026 would let pharma marketers commit budgets again, which is the core of the recovery case.
