Thesis

Pediatrix is the national leader in neonatology and maternal-fetal physician services. Hospitals need staffed NICUs, and direct competition in this niche is weak. The company spent 2 years exiting unprofitable office-based practices, and the streamlined hospital-based core now shows in the numbers. Q1 2026 adjusted EBITDA grew to $58.2M from $49.2M a year earlier, and management reaffirmed full-year adjusted EBITDA guidance of $280M to $300M on May 5, 2026.

At $26.76 the market cap is $2.15B. With roughly $591M of debt less cash, enterprise value sits near $2.6B, about 8.7x to 9x the $290M guidance midpoint. Trailing P/E is 12.96 and forward P/E is 11.80. Physician-services peers have historically traded at 10x to 12x EBITDA, and Pediatrix sits well below its own pre-2020 multiple range.

Our verification pass confirmed every core number against SEC filings and earnings transcripts. It also flagged the honest problem: the stock is up around 86 percent over 12 months and sits near its 52-week high. The cheapness leg has partly played out. We still like the position because the earnings power of the streamlined core supports the current multiple with room to re-rate.

Business

Pediatrix staffs and manages physician groups in hospitals across the United States. Its neonatologists run neonatal intensive care units, and its maternal-fetal medicine specialists manage high-risk pregnancies. Hospitals cannot easily replicate this staffing in-house, which gives Pediatrix durable contract relationships. The company also provides pediatric cardiology and other pediatric subspecialty services.

The old Pediatrix carried a sprawl of office-based practices that diluted margins. Management divested or exited those lines and refocused on the hospital-based core. Same-unit revenue and margin now grow, and Q1 2026 adjusted EPS of $0.44 beat the $0.38 consensus. Revenue over the trailing 12 months was $1.93B.

Why the market is wrong

Screens still price Pediatrix off its old bloated cost structure. The multiple gap versus physician-services peers, 8.7x here against a historical 10x to 12x for the group, reflects skepticism that the turnaround holds. Q1 answered part of that: EBITDA up 18 percent year over year, an EPS beat, and reaffirmed guidance.

We will state the counterpoint plainly. The market has already corrected much of this. Consensus rates the stock a Buy, yet the average price target of $23.17 sits below the current price. Our variant view is that the targets lag the fundamentals. If the company delivers the guided $280M to $300M and the multiple drifts toward peer levels, the stock has meaningful upside from earnings power alone. This memo is a bet on execution at a fair price, with the neglect discount mostly gone.

Valuation

Enterprise value of roughly $2.6B against $290M of guided EBITDA gives 8.7x to 9x. A re-rate to 10x EBITDA, the low end of the peer range, implies an enterprise value near $2.9B and an equity value around $29 per share, roughly 8 percent upside before any EBITDA growth. At 11x the stock reaches the mid $30s. On earnings, 11.8x forward P/E is undemanding for a business growing same-unit revenue with a national franchise.

The original thesis cited a market cap of about $2.0B. That figure is stale. The correct number as of July 2, 2026 is $2.15B, which nudges the EV/EBITDA multiple from about 8.5x to the 8.7x to 9x range. The valuation case survives the correction but with a thinner margin of safety than when the thesis formed.

Risks

Payer mix drives everything. Medicaid covers a large share of NICU patients, so state Medicaid rate cuts hit EBITDA directly. Management flagged healthcare subsidy lapse and Medicaid payer-mix risk on the last call. Declining US birth rates pressure NICU volumes over time, and the company already reports modest volume declines with a flat pricing outlook.

Two verification caveats matter. First, the Q1 beat included revenue-cycle-management cash-collection benefits that management said will tail off, so Q1 may overstate the run rate. Second, our verifiers confirmed the ~$591M debt figure only from thesis math and secondary sources, and did not tie it to the latest 10-Q balance sheet, so verification confidence is medium. The thesis dies if the 86 percent run already priced in the turnaround and volumes keep sliding. At 9x EBITDA there is less cushion for a guidance cut than there was at $15.

Catalysts

Q2 2026 earnings arrive August 4, 2026. A second consecutive quarter of EBITDA growth without the RCM tailwind would prove the run rate and likely force analyst targets above the current price. Any guidance raise toward the top of the $280M to $300M range re-rates the stock. Further portfolio cleanup or capital return announcements would add support. Clarity on state Medicaid budgets through the fall removes the largest overhang, in either direction.
