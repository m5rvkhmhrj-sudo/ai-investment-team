Crown Crafts: an 11 percent yield the screens still price as a money-loser

Thesis

Crown Crafts dominates a small, unglamorous niche: infant and toddler soft goods sold into Walmart, Amazon, and Target. The stock trades at $2.94, a $29.4M market cap, and 0.36x sales, with no analyst coverage. The board pays a $0.08 quarterly dividend, an 10.9 to 11.6 percent yield, and held it through a full retail reset.

The neglect is visible on the quote page. Most screens still show a negative trailing P/E near -3x. That number is stale. FY25 absorbed a $13.8M non-cash goodwill impairment. FY26, which ended March 29, 2026, earned $1.8M in net income, or $0.17 diluted. Q4 swung to $0.3M of net income from a $10.8M loss a year earlier, and gross margin jumped to 22.9 percent from 18.3 percent. Screens have not caught up, so quantitative buyers skip the stock.

We underwrite a business that generated more than $8M of operating cash flow in FY26, paid down $4.4M of debt, and kept the dividend. If normalized earnings reach the $4M to $6M range as margins recover, the stock trades at 5x to 7x. On actual FY26 earnings it trades near 16x, so the cheapness depends on that recovery. We size the position for that uncertainty.

Business

Crown Crafts, based in Gonzales, Louisiana, designs and sells infant and toddler products: crib bedding, blankets, bibs, bath items, diaper bags, and developmental toys. It sells under owned brands Sassy and Manhattan Toy and under licensed brands, including Disney properties. Product is sourced mostly from China and sold through mass retail, with Walmart and Amazon as the largest customers.

The category is a duopoly-like niche. Volumes are too small to attract large consumer-products companies, and licensed retail relationships take years to build. That structure has let Crown Crafts hold shelf space for decades. FY26 net sales were $82.3M, down from $87.3M in FY25 as retailers destocked, but Q4 sales of $22.4M came with the strongest gross margin in 2 years.

Why the market is wrong

The market prices Crown Crafts off a headline that no longer exists. The negative trailing P/E on quote sites reflects the FY25 impairment, a non-cash accounting event, and ignores that all of FY26 was profitable. With zero analysts and a $29M cap, nobody is paid to correct the record. That gap is our edge.

Our variant view rests on cash, and we state it honestly. Reported net income of $1.8M does not cover the $3.4M annual dividend. Operating cash flow of more than $8M covers it roughly 2.4 times, funded the $4.4M debt paydown, and reflects working capital release plus non-cash charges. A board that maintained the payout through the impairment year, and declared the July 2, 2026 dividend on schedule, is signaling it expects earnings to normalize toward the historical $4M to $6M range. Crown Crafts itself averaged 8x to 12x earnings and a mid-single-digit yield over the past decade. Either earnings recover and the stock re-rates, or the yield alone pays us double digits while we wait.

Valuation

At 0.36x sales, Crown Crafts trades far below small consumer-products peers at 10x to 15x earnings and near 1x sales. On FY26 reported EPS of $0.17 the multiple is about 16x, so the sales multiple carries the valuation case today. If gross margin holds near the Q4 level of 22.9 percent on flat revenue, net income in the $4M to $6M range is achievable, which puts the stock at 5x to 7x forward earnings.

A return to the company's own 10-year average of 8x to 12x on $4M of earnings implies a $32M to $48M value, or $3.00 to $4.50 per share, plus the $0.32 annual dividend. The downside anchor is the yield: at $2.94 the payout returns almost 11 percent of the purchase price each year, as long as it survives. We flag clearly that the original screen described TTM EPS as negative; verification corrected this, FY26 EPS was positive $0.17, which strengthens the neglect argument and weakens the headline earnings multiple.

Risks

Three things kill this thesis. First, tariffs. Product is China-sourced, and management flagged US tariff risk in the FY26 filing. A new tariff round compresses the gross margin recovery we underwrite. Second, customer concentration. Walmart and Amazon dominate sales, and a lost program or a retailer-forced price reset flows straight to earnings. Third, the balance sheet. The company carries $14.1M of total debt against only $0.2M of cash, on a $29M market cap. That leverage leaves little room for error.

The dividend is the visible pressure point. The $3.4M annual payout is roughly 185 percent of FY26 net income and is covered only by operating cash flow, part of which came from working capital release that will not repeat every year. If margins compress again, the board cuts, the yield support vanishes, and a $29M micro-cap with thin float can fall fast. Our verification team rated the thesis medium confidence for exactly this reason: the facts check out, but the valuation cushion is thinner than the original screen claimed, and the $4M to $6M normalized earnings figure remains unproven.

Catalysts

The July 2, 2026 dividend payment already confirmed the board's commitment for another quarter. Q1 FY27 results, due around August 2026, will show whether the 22.9 percent Q4 gross margin holds; 2 more quarters at that level roughly proves the normalized earnings case. Quote-site trailing P/E turns positive as the FY25 impairment quarter rolls out of the TTM window, which puts the stock back on value screens. Continued debt paydown at the FY26 pace of $4.4M per year shifts value from creditors to a $29M equity quickly. Tariff clarity, in either direction, ends the overhang that keeps buyers away.
